## How Medicare helps pay for a GP visit

The Medicare Benefits Schedule (MBS) is the government's list of services Medicare helps pay for. Each service on it has a set fee, called the schedule fee [1]. Doctors can charge more than this, and you pay the difference [1].

A Medicare rebate is the part of the fee Medicare covers. There are three common ways this works:

- **Bulk billing:** Medicare pays the doctor directly and you pay nothing [1] [2].
- **Pay, then claim:** you pay the full fee, then claim back the part Medicare covers. Some clinics make the claim for you [1] [2].
- **Pay the gap:** you pay only the difference between the fee and what Medicare covers [1] [2].

It's a good idea to check what you'll pay when you book [1].

## Telehealth and the 12-month rule

Telehealth means seeing a health worker by phone or video call [5]. Medicare can pay rebates for both video and phone appointments [3]. But GP telehealth has an extra rule.

For a GP video or phone appointment to get a Medicare rebate, one of these usually needs to be true [3] [4]:

- you've had a face-to-face Medicare appointment with that GP, or at that practice, in the last 12 months
- you're registered with that practice through MyMedicare, and the appointment is with that practice

MyMedicare is a way of registering with your usual practice [3] [6]. Past video or phone calls don't count as face to face [4]. This rule used to be called the "established clinical relationship" rule [4].

## Who doesn't need to meet the rule

Some people are exempt from the 12-month rule [3] [4]. This includes people who:

- are under 12 months old
- are experiencing homelessness
- live in an area declared a natural disaster area
- are seen at an Aboriginal Medical Service or Aboriginal Community Controlled Health Service
- must isolate for COVID-19 under a public health order (only when one is in place)

Some kinds of appointment are exempt too [3] [4]:

- urgent after-hours care at unsociable hours
- some mental health treatment services
- eating disorder care
- making or reviewing a GP chronic condition management plan
- blood-borne virus, sexual or reproductive health care, but not fertility treatment or pregnancy check-ups

The doctor has to record which exemption applies and why [3].

## Why new patients of a telehealth service usually pay privately

A telehealth service hasn't seen a new patient face to face. So the 12-month rule usually isn't met, unless an exemption applies [3] [4]. GPs can only use the Medicare telehealth items when the rule is met [3].

That means the appointment usually can't be claimed through Medicare. Instead, you pay a private fee with no rebate.

## What changes if you're seen in person

The 12-month rule is about telehealth [3]. A face-to-face GP visit isn't affected by it. So a Medicare rebate can apply to an in-person appointment in the usual way [1].

## GP chronic condition management plans

A GP chronic condition management plan (GPCCMP) is a plan to support people with one or more long-term health conditions [6]. It replaced GP Management Plans and Team Care Arrangements on 1 July 2025 [6].

Where you get a plan depends on MyMedicare [6]:

- **Registered with MyMedicare:** through the practice where you're registered.
- **Not registered:** through your usual GP.

## Medicare rebates for some allied health visits

Allied health means health workers such as psychologists, dietitians, occupational therapists and physiotherapists [7].

With a GPCCMP, your GP can refer you to them. Some of these visits attract a Medicare rebate [7]. Here's how it works:

- Your GP decides whether allied health would help your condition [7].
- The visits need to fit with your plan [7].
- Most people can get up to 5 individual visits with a rebate each calendar year. Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people can get up to 10 [7].
- To keep access, your plan needs to have been made or reviewed in the last 18 months [6].

Allied health workers can charge more than the schedule fee. If they do, you pay the difference [1].

## Questions to ask before you book

- Is this appointment by video, phone or in person?
- Will a Medicare rebate apply, and what will I pay [1]?
- Am I registered with MyMedicare, and where?
- Could a GP chronic condition management plan help me?
