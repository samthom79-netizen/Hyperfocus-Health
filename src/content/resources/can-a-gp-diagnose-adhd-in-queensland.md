## The answer in one line

Yes, many can. In Queensland, specialist GPs can assess, diagnose and manage ADHD in adults, though not every GP chooses to [1].

It is up to each specialist GP to decide if they can offer this [1]. So the real question is whether your GP does. This page helps you find out.

## What is a specialist GP?

A GP (general practitioner) is a family doctor. A specialist GP is a doctor who has done extra training and holds specialist qualifications [1].

Not sure if your doctor is a specialist GP? You can simply ask them [1].

## Why not every GP offers it

ADHD assessment and care usually need longer, more complex appointments [1]. Those appointments may cost more [1].

Each specialist GP decides if they can provide this [1]. A GP who doesn't is not turning you away for good. If your doctor can't help, they may point you to a local specialist GP or another medical specialist who can [1].

They may also refer you to another qualified practitioner [1], such as:

- a psychiatrist
- a psychologist
- a paediatrician

## When you might see a psychiatrist

A psychiatrist is a medical doctor with specialised training in diagnosing and treating mental, emotional and behavioural conditions [5]. If you need to see one, your GP can give you a referral [5].

Even a specialist GP may still refer you to a psychiatrist for assessment, diagnosis or ongoing care [1]. This is more likely if you have other medical conditions [1].

There is a good reason for this. Other things can cause trouble with focus, restlessness or acting on impulse [2]. And a diagnosis of ADHD should prompt a check for other conditions too [3].

## What a diagnosis involves

Whoever you see, a proper assessment matters if your symptoms keep going [1]. Having symptoms of ADHD does not always mean you will be diagnosed with it [1].

A diagnosis involves clinical interviews with a health professional, plus medical and psychological checks [2]. The person assessing you should be trained in diagnostic assessment [3].

### What the assessment usually covers

- **Your story.** Your development, family, health, school and work history [4].
- **Your childhood.** For an adult diagnosis, your symptoms need to have started in childhood. You may be asked for old school reports [2].
- **People who know you.** Your doctor might talk to a partner or parent about how things affect daily life [2].
- **Questionnaires.** Rating scales help, but they work alongside an interview, not instead of one [4].
- **Your physical health.** A medical check looks for things that can add to symptoms, such as sleep problems [4].
- **Other conditions.** The clinician should look at other conditions that could explain or sit alongside your symptoms [3].

### The criteria, in plain English

Doctors use clear criteria to diagnose ADHD [2]. In short, symptoms must have:

- lasted more than 6 months [2]
- been present before age 12 [2]
- caused real problems in at least 2 parts of life, such as home and work [2]

## What to ask your own GP

Your GP is a good first step [1]. Write these questions down before you go, so you don't have to remember them on the spot.

1. "Are you a specialist GP?" [1]
2. "Do you assess and diagnose ADHD in adults?" [1]
3. "If not, can you suggest a local specialist GP or another doctor who does?" [1]
4. "Will I need longer appointments, and what will they cost?" [1]
5. "With my other health conditions, would you suggest I see a psychiatrist?" [1]
6. "What should I bring?" School reports can help [2].

If the answer to question 2 is no, that's fine. Ask question 3 next.
